# Why Little Wins?

Little Wins started at home, not as a product idea. This page is the story behind it, and why it
works the way it does.

## A child who was stuck

Our son was having a hard time: following instructions, respecting adults, getting along with others. We couldn't
find a way to help him change. So, with his counselor, we started trying different methods.

## What we tried first

We counted good actions by hand. We filled a pot with marbles. Each worked for a little while, and then it
stopped sticking. He learned to game the system: a marble was a marble, whatever it took to get one, and a
counting method that's easy to game teaches the wrong lesson.

Before writing any code, we made a paper version of what became Little Wins, to see whether the ideas held up.

## Bring them back to the positive

With a child like ours, a lot of the work is pulling him back to positive thinking. A chart covered in what
went wrong does the opposite: it becomes one more place where he's failing.

So Little Wins shows children only the good news: what they earned, their stars, and how close the next reward
is. Penalties, warnings and our notes are there for us parents, so we can keep track and stay consistent
with each other, but he never sees them in the app.

The balance also never goes below zero. A bad day can take points away, but it can't dig a hole he has to
climb out of before anything good counts again.

## Points now, stars later

Points are immediate: do something good, see it count. That matters, but it's only half of what we wanted. We
also wanted him to build a longer view: to understand that what he does today has consequences over time.

That's what the weekly challenges and their stars are for. A challenge wins a star for the week with at least
two perfect days and no bad day. Two perfect days, not seven, because a rule that asks for perfection is a
rule a child gives up on by Tuesday. And no bad day, because one day really can cost something that took a week
to build. The bigger rewards need stars, not just points, so the long run is visible on his screen: a ring
filling up, week after week.

## From a test to something to share

I built the first version partly to see what Claude, an AI coding assistant, would make of it. The result was
better than I expected, even that first version, which was quite different from today's. My wife loved it. His
counselor loved it. We started using it every day.

That's when it seemed worth making it available to more families, in case it helps someone else. It's free
and open source, and it runs on your own server, because a chart about a child's behavior is about as private
as family data gets. There are no ads, no trackers and no subscription, and your children never need an
account.

## Two months in

We've been using Little Wins for about two months now, and we're seeing progress. It isn't revolutionary.
It's small progress, a little win at a time, and we'll take every one of them.

## Is it for you?

If you're a parent facing something similar, a child who's stuck in a negative loop that the usual methods
don't break, I'd encourage you to try it, ideally alongside whoever is already helping your child, such as a
counselor or a teacher. I can't promise results. Every child is different, and an app doesn't replace the
work. But it gave us a simple, positive way to notice the good, every day, and to show it to him.
